# SporeBot Use and Privacy Notice

## Summary

The short version. The full notice below is what you accept.

- **SporeBot is a research assistant.** It answers your questions from a library of published reports, public websites and our own analysis, and shows the sources for every answer. Check the cited sources before you rely on an answer: it is generated by an AI model and can be wrong.
- **Your employer is in charge of your account.** It signed up for SporeBot and invited you. It decides who can use it, which content you see and how long your conversations are kept. We work on its instructions.
- **Your conversations are kept for 30 days at most.** Your employer can shorten that, down to keeping no history at all. Audit records (who signed in, when a question was asked, which sources were cited, what was downloaded) are kept for 12 months and never contain the text of a question or an answer.
- **Everything is hosted in Paris, France**, by Scaleway. Questions are answered by AI models run by Scaleway in Paris. No name, email address or account identifier is ever sent to a model.
- **Your questions are never used to train any AI model**, never sold, and never shown to another customer. Our staff do not read conversations unless your employer asks for support on one; such access is logged.
- **Models served outside the EU are off** unless your employer signs for them. If it does, those models are labelled in the model picker and your conversation content (still without identifiers) may be processed outside the EU under the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, with zero data retention.
- **Your rights:** access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability and objection. For your account and conversations, ask your employer first; we help it answer. For anything we control, write to privacyanddata@myceliumgastronomy.com.
- **This notice is in use during the test phase** and is under legal review. A reviewed version will replace it, and you will be asked to accept the new version.

### Part A — Users of SporeBot and people who contact us

#### 1. Who decides about your data

If you use SporeBot, your employer (our customer) signed up for it and invited you. **Your employer is the controller** of your account, your sign-in events, your questions and your conversations. We process that data **on your employer's behalf and on its instructions**, under a data processing agreement. For requests about that data (access, correction, deletion), contact your employer first; we will help it answer.

**We are the controller** only for:

- our own operational and security logs of the service (technical metadata, no question or answer text);
- the library of public sources (Part B);
- messages you send us directly (support, privacy, security or takedown requests).

The rest of Part A describes both, so that you can see the whole picture.

#### 2. What we process on your employer's behalf

| Data | What it is | How long it is kept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account | Email address, name if given, role, status, last sign-in time and method, the version of this notice you accepted and when | While your employer uses the service |
| Sign-in | Single-use sign-in links (valid 15 minutes) or your employer's single sign-on, session tokens, sign-in and sign-out events, the IP address at sign-in | Links expire after 15 minutes; events as audit records, below |

#### 4. Where your data is processed and who receives it

Everything we store is hosted by **Scaleway in Paris, France**: the application, the database, the search index, the files you can download, our logs and backups.

By default, questions are answered by AI models run by **Scaleway in Paris** (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Qwen 3.8 27B). What the model receives: our fixed instructions, your workspace's instructions written by your employer, excerpts from the library, recent turns of the conversation and your question. **No name, email address or account identifier is sent to the model.**

Sign-in and service emails are sent by **Scaleway Transactional Email in Paris**. It receives your email address and the email itself, nothing else. If your employer uses single sign-on, its identity provider (for example Microsoft Entra ID or Google Workspace) handles your sign-in under your employer's own terms.

**External models (only if your employer chose them).** Your employer can ask us to enable Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol. We do this only after your employer has signed for it. If you then pick one of these models for a conversation, that conversation's content (as above, still without identifiers) is sent through OpenRouter's EU endpoint to Amazon Web Services (for Claude) or Microsoft Azure (for GPT). These providers may process it outside the EU, including in the United States. The model picker labels these models "served outside the EU". The transfer relies on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, and every request asks the providers to keep nothing (zero data retention) and not to use it for training. Where your employer has chosen these models, they may also be used as a backup when the Paris provider is unavailable. Today external models are not enabled for any customer.

The full list of companies that process data for us is available on request at privacyanddata@myceliumgastronomy.com.

#### 5. Your rights

You can ask for access to, correction or deletion of your data, restriction of processing, data portability, and you can object to processing based on legitimate interests. For data we process for your employer, we pass your request to your employer and help it respond. For data we control, write to privacyanddata@myceliumgastronomy.com; we answer within one month.

You can also complain to a data protection supervisory authority, in particular in the EU country where you live or work. Ours is the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (AEPD), Spain.

#### 6. Security

Each customer's data is kept apart by database rules that are tested on every change. Logs never contain your questions or answers. Sign-in links work once, for 15 minutes, only after you press a button on the page they open, and only in the browser that asked for them. Administrators use a second factor. Data is encrypted in transit. Details are in our security documentation, available to customers on request.

### Part B — People named in the public sources in our library

#### 7. Why you may be in our library

Our library contains published third-party material (research reports, industry studies, company publications, public websites) and our own analysis of it. Published material often names people: authors, executives, spokespeople, people quoted or mentioned. We did not get this information from you, so GDPR Article 14 applies. **We are the controller** of this data.

#### 8. What we hold

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Categories | Names, job titles, employers, professional statements and quotes, and other information that appears in the published text about a person in a professional or public capacity |
| Not intended | Special categories (health, political opinions, religion and similar). We do not seek them out, and web pages likely to contain them are skipped automatically when we crawl a site |
| Sources | Publicly available reports and documents from their publishers, and public websites that do not opt out of text and data mining (we respect robots.txt and the machine-readable reservation under EU copyright law). A summary of the publishers in our library is available on request at privacyanddata@myceliumgastronomy.com |
| What we do with it | Split the text into short passages, index them for search, show passages with their source (publisher, title, date, page) to our customers' users when relevant to a question, and offer downloads of a file only where its licence allows |
| Who sees it | Users of our business customers, through answers and citations. The text is also sent, as excerpts, to the AI model answering a question (section 4) |

#### 9. Purpose and legal basis

Purpose: providing a research service that helps businesses find and cite published information.

Legal basis: our **legitimate interests** and those of our customers in research based on published sources (Art. 6(1)(f)). We assessed that the use is limited to what was already published, in its professional context, and that it is proportionate. The assessment is available on request.

#### 10. Why we publish this notice instead of contacting you

The library contains many documents from many publishers, and they name many people in passing. We usually have no contact details for them, and finding and contacting each would require a disproportionate effort compared with the limited impact of indexing what is already published (Art. 14(5)(b)). We therefore make this notice public, keep a register of every source, and act quickly on requests.

#### 11. How long we keep it

As long as the source stays licensed and relevant to our library. Each source has a review date in our register. When a source is withdrawn, its passages are removed from every search index and cached answer; answers already given keep their citation, marked as withdrawn, and where the withdrawal is for licence reasons the quoted excerpt is removed too.

#### 12. Your rights and how to ask for removal

You can ask what we hold about you, ask for correction, **object** to our use of it, or ask for its **erasure**. We assess each request and, where it succeeds, remove the document or replace it with a copy without your data **within 30 days**, and tell you what we decided and why.

Write to privacyanddata@myceliumgastronomy.com, naming the document or web page if you can. We may ask for information to confirm the request concerns you.

Publishers who want their content removed can use the same address. We act on publisher takedown requests within 10 working days.

You can also complain to a supervisory authority (section 5).
